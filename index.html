“To come home to a quiet room, to shut the door on thenoisy world and feel the familiar walls close around youlike an old coat, is to know a specific kind of grace. It isthe place where you can finally cast off your armor.”
May Sarton · Journal of a Solitude
We asked twenty-two people what home means. 
A flat in a building from the 1600s. A wheat field in Idaho with the horizon for a comfort blanket. A house sold two years ago and still walked through with eyes closed. The shore of Lake Superior. A closet to cry in.
What came back wasn’t a place but a way of being held — by walls, by weather, by people long gone. Some arrived as an essay, some as a poem. One arrived shaped like the word itself, repeated down the page until it came apart.
LIV is an independent magazine made by a mother and a son over one very long year. Forty-eight pages, printed on stock you can feel. No advertising, no listicles, nothing to scroll past. This is Issue 01, and there will be a second.
Alex
Cohen
For me, home is a paradox. It contains both the buzziest energies of creation and the deepest trenches of calm.
Here, I am free to occupy any form, take any shape. I dress and I dance and I lay about for many hours. Home is the freedom to choose between hiding and infinity.
A place to be with my love and the ones I love. A place to rest and sew up my heart on the hardest days. A closet to cry in and wallow with. A desk to draw on, a window to open, a bed to break in.
Home is not the walls that keep the world out, it’s the world that we keep our hearts in.
